01 — The Thesis

Why this works
where digital doesn't.

LongFi's category sits in an awkward place for traditional outbound. The product is technical enough that prospects want to be educated before a conversation. The buyer is senior enough that they don't take cold outreach seriously. And the deal size is large enough that trust matters more than reach. Technical, senior, trust-gated — that combination breaks digital-only marketing and rewards network-led partnership.
What doesn't work
Pure digital marketing into this ICP.
  • Portfolio managers don't read cold LinkedIn DMs
  • Franchise operators don't click paid ads at the office
  • Multi-unit decision-makers don't respond to spray-and-pray sequences
  • The buyer pool is too small and too well-defended for reach-based outbound
  • By the time inbound shows up, the deal is usually already decided
What does
Senior operators with real networks, supported by trust-building content.
  • A warm introduction from someone the buyer already trusts beats any cold message ever sent
  • Content that pre-educates raises the floor of the first conversation
  • Content that handles objections dismantles the most common pushbacks before they're raised
  • Once a key contact says yes, the portfolio dynamic compounds — one decision, many sites
  • Network access is the product. Content is the catalyst.
The pattern that proves it
One rep. Months of groundwork. One key contact at a restaurant group. Thirty-plus locations from a single relationship. That isn't anomaly — that's the shape of how LongFi's market actually opens.
The mistake most agencies make is treating that pattern as a story to tell. We're treating it as a system to replicate. Two operators, two networks, two regions — running the same play simultaneously, with content engineered to make every conversation start ten steps further along than it otherwise would.

Why Canada
changes the math.

🇨🇦 The Canadian Opportunity
A new market.
A network already inside.

You asked specifically for a Canadian market focus. Here's why we think it deserves its own arm of the strategy, not a side note.

Canada is greenfield for LongFi. The carrier-offload model has near-zero competitive penetration north of the border, and the Canadian telecom market is famously over-priced and under-served. Restaurant groups, entertainment venues, and hospitality operators in Montreal, Toronto, and Vancouver are paying premium rates for connectivity that doesn't meet their needs — exactly the wedge LongFi solves in the US.

The difference: Daniel is already inside. He's spent years filming in the venues that map directly to your ICP — restaurant groups, nightlife, entertainment. Those owners know him by name. That's not "we'll build a network" — that's "the network already exists, we're activating it."
